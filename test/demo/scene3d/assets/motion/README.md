# Motion fixtures

These fixtures are dedicated to the public domain under CC0. `_generate.py` regenerates every file except `kenney/` with Python 3's standard library; `_oracle.mjs` regenerates `oracle.json`.

- `jump.bvh`: a 22-joint humanoid with CMU-style joint names, in centimetres, jumping for one second at 30 frames per second. The root has six channels and every other joint three, all in `Zrotation Xrotation Yrotation` order; the toes, head and hands end in End Sites.
- `expected.json`: world joint positions in centimetres at frames 0, 8, 15 and 30, computed by the generator's own forward-kinematics pass from the same source description. `test/lambda/scene3d/motion_bvh.ls` checks the BVH converter and `motion.pose` against them.

- `jump.fbx`, `jump-7500.fbx`, `jump-ascii.fbx`: the same skeleton and jump as FBX — binary 7.4 with raw arrays, binary 7.5 (64-bit record headers) with zlib-compressed arrays, and ASCII 7.4. Each joint is a `LimbNode` model in centimetres with `RotationOrder` YXZ (BVH's `Zrotation Xrotation Yrotation`), and one `AnimationStack` holds linear `Lcl Rotation` curves for every joint plus `Lcl Translation` curves for the hips at 30 frames per second. A skinned body of one box per bone segment carries two Lambert materials; the head's material embeds an 8×8 PNG, red above blue, in its `Video` content. `test/lambda/scene3d/motion_fbx_raw.ls` checks that the three raw trees agree; `motion_fbx.ls` checks the converted characters against the BVH and the reference (vibe/Lambda_Pkg_Motion.md §4, §7).
- `rig.fbx`: a Z-up file in inches whose six-joint rig exercises the transform chain (rotation offset and pivot, pre- and post-rotation, scaling offset and pivot, non-uniform scale, YZX and ZYX rotation orders), a `Null` root, a skin bound with one joint bent away from its rest pose, a vertex with five influences, a geometric translation, two materials, a rigid prop under the hand, a camera, cubic keys, a constant step and a 350° Euler turn, in three stacks (one duplicate name).
- `expected-fbx.json`: the generator's own FBX evaluation of both files, in character metres: skinned jump vertices at frames 0 and 15, and the rig's joint positions, skinned vertices and prop corners at rest and half a second into its clips. Skinned vertices are keyed by their rest position in millimetres.
- `giant.fbx`, `apose.fbx`: the jump's skeleton under Mixamo names (`mixamorig:` prefix, `Spine`/`Spine1`/`Spine2` for CMU's `LowerBack`/`Spine`/`Spine1`), with no clips. The giant is 1.5 times larger with the same rest rotations; the A-pose has the jump's proportions with the upper arms lowered 40° at rest. `test/lambda/scene3d/motion_ops.ls` attaches the jump to both: the giant takes the copy path and the A-pose the retarget path (vibe/Lambda_Pkg_Motion.md §8.3, §8.4).
- `offsets.bvh`: a three-joint chain whose root OFFSET is nonzero and whose middle joint has position channels, so reading position channels as replacing OFFSET or adding to it differs. Three.js `BVHLoader` adds them, and so does the converter.
- `twisted.fbx`: the jump's T-pose under Mixamo names with every joint's local frame turned about Z. The world skeleton is the jump's, but the bone offsets point other ways, so `attach` must retarget rather than copy, and the retargeted world motion equals the source's.
- `kenney/`: a third-party CC0 character, Kenney's [Animated Characters Protagonists](https://kenney.nl/assets/animated-characters-protagonists) 1.1 (`kenney_animated-characters-protagonists.zip`, sha256 `ec3787de70fa2200256848d74201b10f6b6c3126594e9857bf989753312c2b84`), unmodified: `Model/characterMedium.fbx`, `Animations/jump.fbx`, `Skins/skaterMaleA.png` and `License.txt`. The binary FBX 7.4 files come from Blender: a 59-joint rig with IK controls under a ×100 `Root`, a skin whose cluster `Transform`s are bone-relative, and an animation file whose rest is itself a pose.
- `oracle.json`: the same files evaluated by independent implementations, in character metres: the pinned Three.js 0.186.1 `BVHLoader`, `FBXLoader` and `SkeletonUtils` (vendored unmodified under `../../vendor/three/addons/`, hashes in its manifest) and ufbx v0.23.1. `test/lambda/scene3d/motion_oracle.ls` compares the converters with them.

To regenerate `oracle.json`, build `_ufbx_dump.c` against ufbx v0.23.1 (`ufbx.c` sha256 `4a956c26a708e40d82ecb0aeaee54da709d5ba7c144c9642174970059bda7e1d`, `ufbx.h` sha256 `f787be529af577efc04f3e3e735844a08556718dbb613d1211c2b313d72895dc`, from `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ufbx/ufbx/v0.23.1/`) into `temp/`, then run `UFBX_DUMP=temp/oracle/bin/ufbx_dump node test/demo/scene3d/assets/motion/_oracle.mjs`. The dump tool is a test-only program and is never linked into `lambda.exe`.

The generated ASCII FBX follows the FBX SDK's layout (`, ` before strings and `,` before numbers, array continuation lines at column 0, a body on every object record) because `FBXLoader`'s ASCII reader depends on it.
