On a quiet morning, the editor opened a document and began checking each sentence. The small dictionary stayed in memory as compact text, while a short index pointed to groups of words. For every token, the checker searched the index and decoded only one line. Common words appeared several times, but this experiment deliberately repeated every lookup without a cache. Three mistakes were included: teh, recieve, and speling. The remaining words described an ordinary writing session with clear language and simple punctuation. After reading the paragraph, the program reported the unknown spellings and measured how long the complete check took.
