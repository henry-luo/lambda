# AUTO MIR L0 benchmark experiment

2026-11-19 · macOS arm64 · Lambda commit `a9443e52e`

L0 reduced the measured MIR compilation phases by about 21% in both runtimes.
I rejected the default switch. The wall-time intervals for the confirmation
set all cross zero, and the sum of median wall times increased in all three
groups when the tuning workloads were included. Explicit MIR selected L2 in
all four explicit-mode probes.

## Method

D8.3.1v3 and D8.4.2 specify AST-first AUTO. D8.3.2 and D8.4.5 leave hotness thresholds to tuning. The control requests MIR L2 with 5 function calls and 10,000 loop back edges. Its ordinary hot images use L1. The candidate requests L0 through the existing level overrides. The profiling patch records levels and timing for Lambda in release builds and keeps Lambda AUTO's fallback link-interface selection independent of the level sweep. JS uses its existing compiler profiling. The pinned executable contains stock MIR. [Formal design](../../doc/Lambda_Formal_Design.md#d83-lambda-execution-tiers)

The benchmark used one macOS arm64 release executable and fresh processes with logging off. Lambda used `LAMBDA_EXEC_BACKEND=auto` and `LAMBDA_MIR_OPT_LEVEL`. LambdaJS used `JS_EXEC_BACKEND=auto` and `js --opt-level=N`. The benchmark driver set the corresponding call and back-edge overrides and `LAMBDA_DISABLE_MIR_CACHE=1`. Profiling ran separately.

The tuning set contained nine workloads with typed Lambda, untyped Lambda, and LambdaJS ports. The driver used two measurement blocks after one warmup, then three blocks to recheck the best call and loop settings. It chose one shared Lambda policy and one LambdaJS policy by geometric mean of wall-time ratios. The confirmation set contained 28 separate workloads with the same three port types. The driver took four interleaved samples per setting after a warmup. The per-process timeout was 8 seconds for tuning and 15 seconds for confirmation.

The comparison checked exit status and exact stdout with only the `__TIMING__:` lines removed. It also rejected the benchmark `FAIL` marker and checked the additional post-timing density oracle for the LambdaJS Navier–Stokes port. Wall time includes the process's whole lifetime. Reported time comes from the workload's own positive timing marker and can include promotion during execution. Aggregate ratios use the geometric mean of the per-row median ratios. The wall intervals resample the interleaved samples and workloads within each engine.
