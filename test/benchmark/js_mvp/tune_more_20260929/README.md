# LambdaJS remaining tuning follow-up, 2026-09-29

This follows the [release-profile comparison and proposal](../../../../vibe/impl/JS_MVP_Release_Profile_Comparison_20260928.md) and the [guarded/runtime follow-up](../tune_remaining_20260929/README.md). The accepted change removes the inferred-pointer-to-tagged-Item conversion from the common ASCII literal `String.prototype.split` result. JavaScript still uses the ordinary `fn_split` and widening path for Unicode and empty-separator cases. The two paths share the literal scan; the Lambda result lane is unchanged. This keeps the JS array representation valid at the language boundary (**S1.11**, **D3.3.1v2**, **D3.3.3v3**) and roots both inputs and the result across allocation (**D5.3.2**). No normative ruling changes.

The exact ordinary-release control is `temp/js-tune-20260928/ascii-decimal-release.exe` (SHA-256 `f684c7a9e967f74b527f7d94fe4294082a12e88adab35bf5de8782f774f6b561`). The split-stage candidate is `temp/js-tune-20260929/final-release.exe` (SHA-256 `d1153d796b0cab80395d8a8e2f866d1c66df96fd1612a3928888fcbfc2325666`). With `JS_EXECUTION_BACKEND=mir`, **21/21** alternating [text/log_pipeline pairs](final-log-21-paired.json) completed with equal stdout and candidate wins. The candidate/control median execution ratio is **0.9887**, with a one-sided 95% paired-bootstrap upper bound of **0.9902**. The earlier split-only [five-pair replay](split-tagged-log-paired.json) was 0.9801 with five wins; a five-pair [split-stage archive replay](final-log-paired.json) was too noisy to establish a gain, which motivated the longer run. The two split binaries differ only in Mach-O UUID and derived signature bytes; their executable payload is byte-identical, as recorded in [validation.json](validation.json). Each JSON retains exact binary and source hashes, sample order, statuses, wall and execution times.

The mixed packed-array arithmetic proposal was implemented as a guarded experiment, then removed. A first admission admitted a named `.length` access into a computed-key reader and failed MIR finalization; a corrected guard and regression fixture passed, including forced GC. The corrected candidate (`temp/js-tune-20260929/array-binary-fixed-release.exe`, SHA-256 `d4b23ed295fc8b2b543d8ac2db52246750f0b97f18357108986f4095fdeaa5b9`) passed the [63-row canonical output screen](array-binary-fixed-canonical-screen.json). Its [three-pair coverage screen](array-binary-fixed-coverage-paired.json) was near flat on `base64`, `triangl`, and `three_way_merge`. Five-pair [control replays](array-binary-controls-paired.json) were flat on `matmul` (**1.0005**, two wins) and slower on `log_pipeline` (**1.0070**, two wins). This does not justify retaining the additional arithmetic guards. Existing guarded numeric stores and self-updates remain in place; the experiment did not alter them. Guard misses and source-order Get remain required by **S1.11** and **D8.2.3**.

Three further release experiments were removed after interleaved exact-archive A/B. A MIR Number guard on boxed local updates (`temp/js-tune-20260929/boxed-update-release.exe`, SHA-256 `36367c3e0fdf6e9fd545fe42622c8eb7139f19a684708abd0d7e143e2565845e`) reduced `triangl`'s dynamic ToNumeric/increment counts from 44.19 million to 1.20 million, but its [five-pair replay](boxed-update-triangl-paired.json) was slower (**1.0163**, zero wins). Moving the Number head into the runtime call (`temp/js-tune-20260929/runtime-update-release.exe`, SHA-256 `a7a7d66f9823b609eb6ad17c142eaeed0f96d7517aa8039eef4713b1d31230eb`) was [flat](runtime-update-triangl-paired.json) (**1.0008**, two wins). Direct Number extraction for string indices (`temp/js-tune-20260929/string-number-release.exe`, SHA-256 `b86ad894722ec221d3c76a5c95e856a038cc8c1ad82be342e14e50e11b756ff7`) slowed [five paired `log_pipeline` runs](string-number-log-paired.json) (**1.0237**, zero wins). Every pair completed with equal normalized stdout. Helper counts alone did not predict speed; none of these three candidates is retained.

Lazily materializing the bulk RegExp loop's final Annex B match string passed the focused protocol and forced-GC tests, but its [41-pair `regexredux` replay](regex-lazy-paired.json) was flat (**0.9981**, 20 wins; one-sided 95% upper bound **1.0180**). The exact candidate is `temp/js-tune-20260929/regex-lazy-release.exe` (SHA-256 `36f69a1b114e4727f620d427f70ac2cecc5d94aec2a2c61846ec1de3e1a79add`); all outputs matched. The bulk path already materializes only the final match, so this extra lazy state is not retained.

The shared literal split scanner was also tried with its known-string pieces appended through the existing verbatim helper instead of the generic spread check. The exact `temp/js-tune-20260929/split-verbatim-release.exe` candidate (SHA-256 `58a08c3b8bb0024c6f63fdf9c67ee43e8e22ee936c0511cf74c3ead1713b2354`) was slower in its [five-pair `log_pipeline` replay](split-verbatim-log-paired.json) (**1.0239**, two wins), with equal output. A guarded ASCII `slice` leaf reused an already-cached two-to-32-byte substring after the original builtin and finite Number indices were proven. Its exact `temp/js-tune-20260929/slice-cache-release.exe` candidate (SHA-256 `caa12d45c5adf8a2dbe2ac2fb5019ba5228677202f647a933a2d4ecae6038cdd`) gave a **1.0041** median ratio with two wins in [five paired `log_pipeline` runs](slice-cache-log-paired.json); output matched, but execution time varied substantially across pairs. Passing the JS substring constructor through the shared literal splitter reused that same cache for split parts, without adding a second scanner. Removing a repeated TypeMap pointer screen after ordinary-map admission was inconclusive on [five Havlak pairs](named-proof-havlak-paired.json): ratio **0.9951**, two wins, and a one-sided 95% upper bound **1.0511**. The exact `temp/js-tune-20260929/named-proof-release.exe` candidate has SHA-256 `4904dddbe90d8c0dc879d231d6a027f02e74148cd19e6ac233df5df7ff801972`; every output matched. It is not retained as a property-path gain.

A `mir_this` call whose receiver and method home exactly matched the installed activation was also tested through the shared one-root call leaf. The nested class-method/private-field regression passed normally and with forced GC, but the exact `temp/js-tune-20260929/call-reuse-release.exe` candidate (SHA-256 `0141e80e1ab4d7837db1b00cf44805b230a1fa6fbf4b978735a6916df447c8c6`) lost four of [five Havlak pairs](call-reuse-havlak-paired.json), ratio **1.0105**, with equal output. The successful named-property and `mir_this` call paths were audited for another removable root or activation step. The current direct call already reuses caller-owned argument roots, while its saved `this` and private home remain live across a potentially allocating body. Removing either needs a transitive effect proof that the present function capability metadata does not supply (**D5.3.2**, **D8.4.1v2**). The current substring path already interns one-byte ASCII strings and caches short repeated substrings, matching the earlier 24.7-million-hit observation; another cache has no established mechanism. These paths remain performance research targets; the measured candidates below did not justify retaining another change.

Validation: the complete `test_js_opt_gtest` suite passed **85/85** after removing the array experiment. The ASCII split regression passed under `LAMBDA_GC_FORCE_EVERY=1 LAMBDA_GC_POISON_FREED=1`. The final-release [63-row canonical screen](final-canonical-screen.json) completed **63/63** with equal normalized stdout. That screen has one pair per row, so only the 21-pair `log_pipeline` replay supports the timing claim. Build, archive, and check provenance are in [validation.json](validation.json).

## Subsequent loop, call, and property screen

A guarded inline-Number lane now handles `+` and `-` between boxed, function-local identifiers in loops. It reuses the existing boxed Number guard, evaluates both operands in source order, and keeps the original `js_add`/`js_subtract` continuation for widened locals, objects, strings, BigInt, and out-of-band Numbers (**S1.11**, **D8.2.3**). The focused MIR fixture contains both `dadd` and `js_add`, and the coercing fallback test passes with forced GC. On the isolated Boyer–Moore diagnostic, dynamic Number arithmetic-head hits fell from **399,349,525** to **169,473,059** with the same checksum. This diagnostic is mechanism evidence; the acceptance evidence is the exact `temp/js-tune-20260929/boxed-loop-pair-release.exe` archive (SHA-256 `9be34d00ae015e1ca89f01f63a6abbaf5bdc6c951952ec0179f7df119ab6f6af`) against the preceding split archive (`d1153d796b0cab80395d8a8e2f866d1c66df96fd1612a3928888fcbfc2325666`): [21/21 `text_search` pairs](boxed-loop-search-21-paired.json) won with equal output, a **0.9767** median execution-time ratio, and a **0.9813** one-sided 95% upper bound. The earlier [five-pair screen](boxed-loop-search-paired.json) agreed.

The no-home entry was reached four million times in its debug mechanism probe. Bounce, CD, Richards, and Havlak reported zero such entries, despite their existing `mir_this` calls; the microbench result is not a claim about those workloads. A typed-array object-key Get bug exposed by the mixed-array probes was separately fixed: Get now performs `ToPropertyKey` at the typed-array exotic boundary before its numeric-index test, just as Set does, so an observable `Symbol.toPrimitive` runs in the required order (**S1.11**). Its normal and forced-GC regressions pass. The broader call, named-property, comparison, split-cache, slice-cache, and RegExp-lazy variants above remain unretained because exact paired evidence did not establish a gain. The retained split and loop-arithmetic paths are the measured follow-up improvements.

The typed-array fix first screened its receiver class on every generic Get. Exact [five-pair control replay](final2-other-control-replay.json) exposed roughly 2% losses in Richards, base64, and Levenshtein; a [five-pair text replay](final2-text-control-replay.json) preserved the search gain but lost `log_pipeline` by **2.09%**. Reordering the predicate recovered most of those losses in [five paired controls](key-order-controls-paired.json), but the ordinary Get still paid for an object-key check. Moving conversion into the typed-array exotic handler removed that check from ordinary receivers. The exact `temp/js-tune-20260929/exotic-key-release.exe` archive (SHA-256 `639b46a92e34ca3a1a2c82df34912425b8bdcbf6207d837b9ba6908dbed56cbd`) passed the object-key, Symbol-key, throwing-coercion, and forced-GC probes. It won [five paired runs against the reordered variant](exotic-key-controls-paired.json) on Richards and was near flat on base64 and Levenshtein; the [direct pre-loop control replay](exotic-key-final-controls-paired.json) was **0.9873**, **0.9967**, and **1.0022** on those rows, with equal output. The [direct five-pair text replay](exotic-key-text-paired.json) gives `text_search` **0.9787** (four wins) and `log_pipeline` **1.0041** (one win); the longer log replay below is the decision gate for that small difference.

The final exact-archive [21-pair `text_search` replay](exotic-key-search-21-paired.json) won **21/21** with equal output: candidate/control median **0.9717**, one-sided 95% upper bound **0.9818** (33,100.4 → 32,163.3 ms). The [21-pair `log_pipeline` replay](exotic-key-log-21-paired.json) was a small measured cost: ratio **1.0029**, upper bound **1.0061**, five wins, equal output (12,362.7 → 12,399.0 ms). The search improvement is roughly 937 ms at these medians, while the log cost is roughly 36 ms. We retain the guarded loop arithmetic and the typed-array key repair, report the log tradeoff, and make no timing claim from the single-pair canonical screen.
