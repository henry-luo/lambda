The final text-search gain is supported by five direct old/final pairs, all faster and output-equal; the one-sided 95% upper bound on its ratio is 0.8222. Triangl's direct old/final screen has noisy individual pairs, but the source-isolated pre-loop/final loop replay is 0.8982, 5/5 faster, with an upper bound of 0.9188. The guarded copy emits a native increment and keeps the original generic sibling. Bounce, NBody, and log pipeline are near flat in this five-pair control screen. The separate [15-pair shared-decoder untyped Lambda replay](shared-unbox-lambda-text-15.json) was 1.0037 (7/15 wins); the final archive's three untyped controls are also near flat, so no Lambda speedup or regression is established.

The comparison correction remains required because the previous raw helper discarded a throwing Error Item. Its MIR-pinned text-search screen is 0.9455 by median with 4/5 faster pairs but a wide uncertainty bound, so no isolated performance claim is made. The early AUTO [text-search](completion-text-five.json), [Bounce](completion-bounce-fifteen.json), and [Triangl](guarded-triangl-five.json) screens are preserved only to explain the backend diagnosis. The [final 63-row screen](final-full-mvp.json) validates 62 same-source full-JS/MVP rows against Node 22.13.0 output and retains Navier–Stokes as an MVP preflight failure. The [generated fixed-population summary](final-summary.json) records a full/MVP execution geomean of 1.550× versus 1.544× in the earlier snapshot, with 17 full-JS wins in each. Its wall geomean is 1.908× versus 1.827×. These one-pair, separate-session ratios describe the two archives on their respective runs; they are not causal attribution. The exact paired screens above carry that claim. Binary and source hashes stayed unchanged during the final screen.

Other source-matched screens stopped extensions that did not earn their cost. A companion packed-array direct reader was [0.9949 on text search](companion-search-v2-five.json), with 4/5 wins but no established removal of the remaining hot checks. Guarded numeric field loads made [NBody 1.0168](field-nbody-five.json) and [Bounce 1.0444](field-bounce-five.json), each with 1/5 wins. The individual-operand exact method ABI was [1.0002 on Richards, 1.0060 on Havlak, and 0.9967 on CD](exact-method-broad-five.json); its [NBody/Bounce control](exact-method-awfy-seven.json) was 0.9984/1.0189. Those candidates were removed. No unguarded field or method assumption was substituted for the original Get and callable identity checks (**S1.11**, **D8.4.2v2**).

The retained runtime work uses the same physical Float decoding in Lambda and JS (**D8.2.3**, **D8.2.6**), admits rooted Float-home computed array keys, preserves holes and inherited indexed reads, and routes coercing binary and branch comparison ERROR Items before a branch consumes their boolean bit (**S1.11**, **D8.4.3v2**, **D5.3**). The exact `Math.max` call checks the retrieved callable after Get and argument evaluation, uses the shared scalar max for two Number arguments, and calls that same retrieved value with rooted original arguments on a miss (**D8.4.2v2**, **D8.4.3v2**). The guarded `while` copy admits only an uncaptured mutable lexical Number with bounded body and local-only updates; a runtime Number guard selects its F64 loop state or the original generic loop (**D8.3.1v2**). The source proof and rejection details are in the proposal document.

[Before/after Triangl MIR evidence](mir-evidence.json) pins both archive and source hashes. The old dump has 5,444 lines, four `dadd` sites, and one `js_increment` call. The final dump has 7,926 lines, six `dadd` sites, and retains one `js_increment` call in the cold generic sibling. The focused MIR regression checks the native increment before the generic call in the same function. On the exact old/final replay, Triangl wall medians are 6,800 → 6,271 ms and text-search wall medians are 35,534 → 28,937 ms. The extra compiled loop body pays for itself on Triangl but makes whole-module MIR larger; the bounded admission rule prevents indiscriminate copying.

For the concrete extract-after-convergence seam, `git diff --numstat` records 62 added lines in `mir_emitter_shared.hpp`, 7 added/30 removed in Lambda's `transpile-mir.cpp`, and 13 added/20 removed in JS's boxing file. The two clients now call `em_unbox_f64_item`; the JS Float-home key lane also calls its non-inline companion. The shared header owns the physical Item bits and emitted branches; each language retains its own cold conversion call. This extraction replaces the two local inline-decoder bodies and avoids duplicating their representation logic (**D8.2.3**).

On the restored guarded-loop source, focused JS optimizer tests passed 91/91; six changed-path cases passed with forced, poisoned GC; the MIR ratchet passed 20/20; `make test-lambda-baseline` passed 6,011/6,011; and `make test262-baseline` passed 40,261/40,261 fully, with zero regressions and zero non-fully-passing cases. The mixed-comparison MIR expectation was updated to require the error-preserving helper. The final Test262 run completed under the normal baseline command, without changing runner exclusions.
